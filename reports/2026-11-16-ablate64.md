## Ablation of 9a1e08fa8d4, pgstat: Allow a kind to release its references after their flush

Each variant is `e9fd4b6` (pg_stat_statements, texts in shared memory) with one
part of `9a1e08fa8d4` removed.  hc100m, 64 vCPUs, 120 s per run.

| variant | commit | removed |
|---|---|---|
| full | `e9fd4b6` | nothing |
| no-post-flush | `885eaa2` | `pgstat_release_flushed_entry_refs()` after `pgstat_flush_pending_entries()` |
| no-idle | `c69851c` | `pgstat_release_flushed_entry_refs()` on the early return with nothing pending |
| no-created | `531a16b` | `pgstat_flush_created_refs()` at the transaction boundary |
| off | `0859df8` | `release_refs_after_flush = false` |
| off-gc | `1de239a` | as off, with `pgstat_request_entry_refs_gc()` when a drop cannot free the entry |

The stale reference replacement in `pgstat_get_entry_ref()` is not ablated: once
references are released, a cached reference may point to a dropped entry, and
a lookup must replace it rather than return it.

### Findings

- **no-created**: at the backstop 84% of the run at 256 clients, 90% at 512;
  at 256 clients allocated
  9735 avg / 10190 max against 4910 / 5141 for full; 39 entries under 1 s old
  against ~970, eviction passes 0.05x upstream.  New statements are not
  tracked, which is why its TPS (5.77x upstream) is higher than full (4.40x).
  Reason: without the flush and release at the end of the creating
  transaction, the creating backend keeps its reference to an entry until the
  next flush, up to a second later.  Under hc100m nearly every unpopular
  statement is a new entry evicted well within that second; the drop cannot
  free an entry still referenced, so dropped entries pile up to twice
  `pg_stat_statements.max`, where new statements are no longer tracked.
- **off**: at the backstop 99% of the run at 256 and 512 clients; at 256, allocated 10067 avg /
  10227 max, no entry under 1 s old, 0 eviction passes; the view stuck at 5099
  from warmup on: nothing is evicted or freed, and its TPS (5.34x upstream)
  is that of not tracking new statements.  Reason: without the option no
  reference is released after a flush or at the end of a transaction, and
  pg_stat_statements does not request a garbage collection of references
  after a drop, so no dropped entry referenced by a backend is ever freed.
- **off-gc**: the same as off, at 256 and 512 clients; at 256: at the backstop 99% of the run,
  allocated 10075 avg / 10237 max, no entry under 1 s old, 0 eviction passes,
  TPS 5.35x upstream from not tracking new statements.  Requesting a garbage
  collection of references after a drop, the approach before the patch, does
  not free the dropped entries.  Likely reason, from the code: a request is
  handled once per backend, in pgstat_gc_entry_refs() on its next lookup,
  which skips the references with pending statistics and then marks the
  request as handled.  Under hc100m an entry is evicted while its creating
  backend still has pending statistics for it, so that reference is skipped
  and not looked at again until another request.  Once the dropped entries
  reach twice `pg_stat_statements.max`, no entry is created, so none is
  dropped and no request comes: allocation stays there.
- **no-post-flush**, **no-idle**: no measurable difference from full at 256
  or 512 clients (at 512: TPS 3.86x and 3.87x against 3.87x, allocated
  4926/5158 and 4896/5157 against 4912/5160, backstop 0%, all 4000 popular
  kept).  Not justified by hc100m: its unpopular statements come from 100M,
  so a backend nearly never holds a reference to an entry another backend
  created, and the references to the entries it created are released at the
  end of the creating transaction.  These two releases are for the other
  references: entries looked up but not created (post-flush), and backends
  with nothing pending, such as idle ones (no-idle).  To test with a
  workload sharing the churning entries across backends (hc1500), and with
  idle sessions.

## How to read the tables

Each point has two tables, one row per build; no pgss is upstream run without
pg_stat_statements loaded.

- **TPS**, and the **speedup over upstream**: 2.0x is twice the TPS of upstream.
- **% of no-pgss**: the TPS of the build as a share of the TPS of no pgss on
  the same instance.
- **CV**: how much TPS varies within the run.
- **TPS trend**: the mean TPS of the last fifth of the run against the first
  fifth; in bold below -5%.
- **top waits**: the most frequent wait events of the active backends, as a
  share of samples, 1% or more; CPU is a backend running, not waiting.
- **allocated avg/max**: entries held by the stats kind, including dropped
  ones not freed yet.
- **recency**: entries of the view by how long ago they were created, on
  average over the run.
- **popular kept**: the fewest of the 4000 popular statements in the view.
- **evictions vs upstream**: eviction passes per transaction, against
  upstream: -27% is 27% fewer.
- **backstop**: share of the run at twice `pg_stat_statements.max`, where new
  statements are not tracked.
- **text / no-text**: size of the query text storage, in the file or in
  shared memory, and the most entries without a text.

Under each point, TPS by fifth of the run for each build, and for the
hotcold workloads the entries of the view by how long ago they were
created.

Workloads: hotcold_100m runs 4000 popular statements and 100 million others;
hotcold_1500 runs 4000 popular statements and 1500 others; select_only runs
pgbench `-S`; SELECT 1 is `SELECT 1;` with the simple protocol.
